The long version

I'm Maxx — a developer, designer, and storyteller who got here by following curiosity instead of a plan.

I started out messing with visual design and storytelling before I ever wrote a line of production code. That order matters: it means I still think like a designer first, even when I'm deep in a database schema or debugging a shader. I care about how things feel, not just whether they compile.

Over the years I moved from freelance design into full-stack development, then into game development — where all of it finally clicked together. Games let me use every skill at once: systems thinking, visual craft, narrative, and the kind of performance tuning that only matters when a player is watching.

I like problems that don't have an obvious answer yet. The messy ones. The kind where the first three ideas are wrong and the fourth one is weird but works. I'd rather ship something small and honest than something big and vague.

Outside of work you'll find me tinkering with side projects, contributing to open source, reading about things I'll probably never build, and writing down what I learn so future-me doesn't forget it.

If you want the short version: I build things that are fast, clear, and a little bit joyful. Everything else is details.

How I work

Four things
I actually believe.

Not values on a wall — the rules I keep coming back to when a project gets hard.

01
🎯

Ship, then refine

A rough thing in someone's hands beats a perfect thing in my head. I build the smallest honest version first, then make it good once it's real.

02
🔍

Details are the product

Fast, obvious, and enjoyable isn't a coat of paint — it's a hundred tiny decisions. I sweat the small stuff because that's what people actually feel.

03
🤝

Clarity beats cleverness

Readable code, plain language, honest estimates. If I have to explain it twice, it probably isn't finished yet.

04
🌱

Stay curious, stay humble

The best idea usually isn't mine — it's the one that shows up after I stop defending my first one. I try to stay wrong long enough to learn something.

FAQ

Things people ask.

If your question isn't here, just email me — I answer everything.

Are you available for freelance work?
Yes — I take on a small number of freelance projects at a time so I can actually give them attention. Send me the scope and timeline and I'll tell you honestly whether it's a fit.
What kind of projects do you enjoy most?
Anything where design and engineering overlap: games, interactive tools, and products where the interface is the experience. I'm less interested in pure CRUD work unless there's something interesting hiding in it.
What's your typical process?
Understand the real problem first (not the one in the brief), sketch the smallest version that could work, build it, then iterate based on how it actually feels. I'd rather show you something rough early than something polished too late.
Do you work with teams or solo?
Both. I've led small teams, mentored juniors, and shipped solo. I like collaborating with designers and product people early — the best outcomes usually come from arguing about ideas before writing code.
How do you handle deadlines?
Honestly. I'd rather tell you a date is slipping two weeks early than pretend everything is fine until it isn't. Clear communication beats heroic last-minute saves every time.
What do you want to build next?
Something that makes people feel something — a game, a tool, a story told in a way that only interactive media can tell it. If that sounds like your project, we should talk.
